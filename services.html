Service routes

Six reasons people usually call.

Each route can be renamed for another trade while preserving the customer-first structure.

01

Power is unreliable

For repeated trips, partial outages, a device or area that stops working, or a condition that changed unexpectedly. Assessment comes before repair promises.

02

More capacity is needed

For added equipment, appliances, workspaces, chargers, renovations or a property whose electrical needs have changed.

03

Lighting is failing or awkward

For failed fixtures, uneven light, new layouts, exterior lighting and controls that need to be easier to use.

04

The panel needs attention

For older equipment, unclear labels, planned additions or a capacity concern. The right recommendation follows an on-site assessment where needed.

05

A renovation needs coordination

For kitchens, additions, tenant work and other projects where scope, access, sequencing and inspections may affect scheduling.

06

Operations are being interrupted

For commercial areas, equipment or circuits affecting staff, tenants or customers. Urgency is triaged by phone; round-the-clock response is not implied.

Scope boundaries

Clear work starts with clear limits.

The written scope should identify included work, exclusions, customer responsibilities, access needs and any authority questions before scheduling.

Assessment first

Remote details help route a request; they do not replace an on-site assessment when one is needed.

Written scope

The customer should be able to see what is proposed before authorizing the work.

No hidden claims

Pricing, warranties, timing and compliance language stay out until the owner can substantiate them.
